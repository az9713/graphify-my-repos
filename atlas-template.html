Correction

The claim I made this morning was wrong

I said your stars land on clones rather than original work. The data says otherwise: derivative repos average 0.68★, originals 0.54★. Both are effectively zero and the gap is noise. Four of the six repos above 10★ are original.

Clustering by subject shows what is actually happening, and it is a sharper problem than the one I described.

The real pattern

Attention is not tracking originality, effort, or difficulty. It tracks subject. Work about agent tooling gets found; everything else does not, no matter how hard it was. lbm-wind-tunnel is not invisible because it is derivative — it isn't derivative. It is invisible because no one browsing GitHub for computational fluid dynamics will ever encounter it.

Neglect

Seventy-two orphans

Repos connected to at most one other repo in the knowledge graph. Not necessarily weak work — several are among the most technically ambitious things you own. They simply sit outside every conversation, including your own.

Alongside these, a blunter discoverability fact: GitHub topics are the main way a repo reaches someone who was not already looking for it. Setting them across the portfolio is an afternoon of scripted work and would plausibly do more for reach than any single project on the suggestions page.

Method & cost

What the expensive step bought, and what it didn't

Graphify ran eighteen parallel subagents over the corpus and consumed input tokens. It produced a graph of 1,569 nodes and 2,868 edges across 544 extracted concepts.

The concepts turned out to be almost entirely tools and vendors — Claude Code, Python, TypeScript, Gemini, Codex, MCP, React. Not one of the top forty is a subject. And 331 of the 544 appear in exactly one repo, so they carry no clustering signal at all. Clustering that graph groups repos by which tools they use, and since nearly everything uses the same three, it produced 146 communities with a median size of 2 and fifty singletons. Useless as an atlas.

Worth saying plainly

The subject clusters on this page did not come from the expensive step. They came from TF-IDF over the README text with the boilerplate stripped — scikit-learn, running locally, at zero token cost, in about two seconds.

My first pass at that also failed: four of fourteen clusters were grouping on README furniture (docs/troubleshooting.md, badge markup) and on the YAML front matter I had added myself. Stripping those fixed it. The lesson is not that graphify is bad — its tool graph is a genuinely useful second layer, and the hub and bridge numbers above come from it. The lesson is that it answered a different question than the one I asked.

Two clusters, holding repos between them, still have generic top terms rather than a subject. That is not a failure of the method. It is the finding: roughly a third of the portfolio is undifferentiated agent infrastructure, similar enough to itself that no algorithm can tell the pieces apart.
